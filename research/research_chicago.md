## 2. Framing of pedestrian safety now
FETCHED facts:
- 2025: 36 pedestrian deaths on surface streets, 2 cyclist, down from 38 and 5 in 2024. Count is from media and preliminary CPD data, not official. Expressways excluded. Streetsblog found mismatches with CDOT's final count.
- About half of 2025 fatal pedestrian crashes were hit-and-runs. Hoodline says about 40 percent of pedestrian incidents downtown.
- Speed is the lead theme. Wide arterials (Ashland had four pedestrian deaths in 2025, also Pulaski, Cicero). Council narrowly rejected cutting the default limit from 30 to 25 mph in Feb 2025.
- Advocate phrases: "still far from Chicago's Vision Zero goals"; streets designed so "ordinary mistakes" are not fatal; people should not need reflective gear to walk to a store; "tragically" in every Streetsblog headline. Active Trans mission: "make walking, biking, and public transit safe and equitable options". Active Trans lead post: 22 alders pushing to keep safe-streets funding.
- Funding: SEARCH-ONLY that the proposed 2025-2029 capital plan cuts Vision Zero and Complete Streets bonds from $40M (2026) to $6M (2029).
- Hoodline (law-firm-sourced, AI-assisted, so treat with care): pedestrian crashes up about 35 percent 2021 to 2025; Loop 129, South Loop 76, West Loop 70, Mag Mile 63. This conflicts in direction with the "deaths fell" story. Crashes and deaths are different measures. Do not mix them in one video.

Fair framing:
- Say "crashes where a person walking or biking was hit" and "police-reported". Name the street type or design, not the neighborhood's people.
- Say "where it happens", not "which ward is dangerous". Point to streets and speeds as the cause.
- Pitfalls: (1) blaming a ward or its residents; (2) downtown exposure, since many victims and drivers do not live there; (3) undercount. SEARCH-ONLY: a past IDOT review found big city/state mismatches, and hospital records show pedestrians missing from police reports, so police counts understate injuries; (4) crashes with injuries vs deaths; (5) hit-and-run records may be incomplete; (6) 2025 data may be revised.
